## 2. Thematic Corpus Assessment

### Theme 1: Peptide Bond Hydrolysis Kinetics & Thermodynamics
- **Foundational Solution Kinetics**:
  - *Radzicka & Wolfenden (1996, JACS)* and *Bryant et al. (1996, JACS)*: Direct measurement of the uncatalyzed neutral water hydrolysis of peptide bonds, establishing spontaneous half-lives of ~400–500 years at room temperature in dilute solution, with free activation energies $\Delta G^\ddagger \approx 120\text{–}130\text{ kJ/mol}$ ($E_a \approx 90\text{–}100\text{ kJ/mol}$).
  - *Smith & Hansen (1998, JACS)* and *Sun et al. (2019, PCCP)*: pH-rate profiles and non-enzymatic cleavage mechanisms demonstrating neutral water attack via zwitterionic/gem-diol intermediates versus base-catalyzed hydroxide attack.
  - *Qian et al. (1993, GCA)*: High-temperature peptide hydrolysis and amino acid decomposition kinetics ($E_a$ determination between 120 and 160 °C).
  - *White (1984, Nature)*: Hydrolytic limits of biomolecules at high temperatures.
  - *Rawitscher, Wadsö & Sturtevant (1961, JACS)*: Microcalorimetric heats of hydrolysis of peptide bonds ($\Delta H \approx -6\text{ to }-10\text{ kJ/mol}$).
  - *Hill (1965, Adv. Protein Chem.)*: Definitive early monograph on acid, base, and neutral protein hydrolysis.

### Theme 2: Collagen Triple Helix & Supramolecular Fibril Mechanochemistry
- **Helical Protection & Steric Hindrance**:
  - *Buhr & Gräter (2026, ACS Omega)*: Decisive computational study showing that the collagen triple helix reduces susceptibility to base-catalyzed hydrolysis. The helical environment raises the activation barrier from 102.5 kJ/mol (single chain) to 111.6 kJ/mol (triple helix), an architectural boost of ~9.1 kJ/mol ($~40\times$ slower hydrolysis), driven by $n \to \pi^*$ orbital interactions and constrained water access.
  - *Flynn et al. (2013, Biomech. Model. Mechanobiol.)* & *Debnath et al. (2024, Soft Matter)*: Single-fibril erosion assays and continuum microarchitecture models demonstrating that axial tension and fibril packing modulate cleavability.
  - *Okada et al. (1992, Biomaterials)*: In vitro/in vivo degradation of collagen sutures as a macroscopic benchmark of collagen proteolysis and hydrolysis.

### Theme 3: Bone Diagenesis, Racemization & Mathematical Degradation Models
- **Thermal Age & Survival Limits**:
  - *Collins et al. (1995, JAS)*: First mathematical simulation of ancient collagen chemical degradation (depolymerization model).
  - *Collins, Waite & van Duin (1999, Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B)*: Predicting protein decomposition using aspartic acid racemization kinetics ($E_a \approx 120\text{–}125\text{ kJ/mol}$).
  - *Collins & Riley (2000)* & *Collins et al. (2002, Archaeometry)*: The foundational reviews on bone diagenesis, concluding that collagen survival is primarily governed by microbial destruction early on, followed by intrinsic chemical hydrolysis whose kinetics depend strictly on the integrated temperature history.
  - *Smith et al. (2001, Nature)*: "Not just old but old and cold?" — establishing that ancient DNA and protein preservation is not a function of absolute chronological age, but effective thermal age.
  - *Wilson et al. (2012, Anal. Chem.)*: Glutamine deamidation kinetics in bone collagen as an intra-crystalline thermal proxy.

### Theme 4: Global Radiocarbon Databases & Curated Chronologies
- **Target Ingestion Repositories**:
  - *XRONOS (Roe et al., 2025, JCAA)*: Modern open-data chronological infrastructure aggregating >100,000 radiocarbon determinations across Europe and globally with structured material tags (`bone`, `charcoal`, `seed`, etc.).
  - *CARD (Canadian Archaeological Radiocarbon Database, Gajewski 2011; Martindale et al.)*: Extensive North American database containing tens of thousands of dated bone and non-bone specimens.
  - *Holarctic Mammal Database (Herrando-Pérez et al., 2026, Sci. Data)* & *Herrando-Pérez et al. (2021, RSOS; 2025, JQS)*: High-quality collagen-purified radiocarbon records with known % collagen yields, C:N ratios, and precise coordinates.
  - *NERD (Palmisano et al., 2022)*, *Aegean (Katsianis et al., 2020)*, *Southern Africa (Loftus et al., 2019)*, *US Lower 48 (Kelly et al., 2022)*, *Pacific (Bickler 2025)*, and *IntChron / IntCal (Bronk Ramsey et al., 2019, 2024)*.

### Theme 5: Palaeoproteomic Yields, Screening & Preservation Thresholds
- **Collagen Recovery Cutoffs**:
  - *Harvey et al. (2016, PLOS ONE)*: ZooMS collagen fingerprinting as a high-throughput screening tool for radiocarbon preservation in archaeological bone.
  - *Talamo et al. (2021)* & *Boudin et al. (2013, RCM)*: Protocols for collagen purification and minimum % yield criteria (>1% wt collagen yield as standard quality threshold for valid dating).
  - *Procopio et al. (2021, JPR)*: Variation of residual non-collagenous bone proteomes as a function of collagen yield.
  - *Peters et al. (2023, Comm. Earth Environ.)*: Preservation of bone collagen for >50,000 years in subtropical Australia, testing the thermal envelope limits.

### Theme 6: Quantum, Molecular Dynamics & Machine Learning Theory
- *David et al. (2024, JACS)*, *Reiher et al. (2017, PNAS)*, *Tesei et al. (2024, Nature)*: State-of-the-art computational workflows for water-mediated reaction path sampling and disordered polypeptide conformational ensembles.

## 3. Key Finding: The Activation Energy Discrepancy

| Context | Activation Energy ($E_a$ or $\Delta G^\ddagger$) | Half-life / Preservation Horizon | Source |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Aqueous Dipeptide (Free solution)** | ~90 – 105 kJ/mol | ~400 – 500 years @ 25 °C | Radzicka & Wolfenden (1996) |
| **Triple Helix (QM/MM simulation)** | 111.6 ± 6.6 kJ/mol | ~15,000 – 20,000 years @ 10 °C | Buhr & Gräter (2026) |
| **Empirical Ancient Collagen Loss (Lab & Site Data)** | **~173 kJ/mol** | **>1,000,000 years @ sub-zero / cold temps** | **Collins Lab Estimate / Pan-Pleistocene Data** |

The ~61 kJ/mol difference indicates that the dense quaternary packing, biomineral encapsulation (hydroxyapatite crystal templating in the gap zone), intra-/inter-molecular cross-linking (DHLNL, Pyridinoline), and restricted water activity ($a_w \ll 1$) within the microfibril confer extraordinary kinetic stability that far exceeds an isolated triple helix.
